# Household D · our house rules

Halina's own rules, in her words. Each has the date it starts.

## D-HR1 · Kuba's share of Ogrodowa

From 2025-07-01.
1. Halina collects Ogrodowa's rent into her account and pays the flat's costs from it: the building fee and any repair.
2. For each calendar quarter from July 2025, Kuba's share is 3/16 of the rent received in the quarter less the flat's costs paid in the quarter, each counted on the day the money moved, to the ort, half up.
3. Halina sends Kuba his share plus whatever was carried in from the quarter before, by the 10th of the month after the quarter (the next business day where the 10th isn't one). Where that sum is below 0, nothing is sent and it is carried into the next quarter.
4. Each of us pays our own tax on our part of the rent.

## D-HR2 · The floor

From 2025-06-13.
1. When the account's balance at the end of any day is below 5 000.00 DUK, and no redemption she has asked for is still unpaid, Halina asks the Treasury on the next business day to redeem bonds.
2. She redeems the earliest series first (by the day they were bought), in whole bonds, as many as it takes for what they pay before the tax (each bond's value less its fee, TSB-4.2) to come to at least 3 000.00 DUK.
3. While a redemption she has asked for is unpaid, she asks for nothing more.

## D-HR3 · The long view

Decided on 2026-09-30. We run the story's long view on paths, and with zero volatility. "Runs dry" is the story's point 8; net worth there is the account plus the fund.

## D-HR4 · How we count what Halina has

From 2024-05-17.
1. Bonds count at what an early redemption asked for that day would pay, after the fee and the tax.
2. A flat counts at its latest valuation by an agent or a bank, for her share. A price the heirs agreed between them is not a valuation.
3. Money owed to her or by her counts at its amount on the day, whether or not it is due yet; a pension owed to her counts at its gross.
4. The house counts at its latest valuation, as a flat does (point 2).
