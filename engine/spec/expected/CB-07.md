Model: `tmp/design/models/CB-07.tally`. Case file: `tmp/verification/cases/CB-07.md`. "Published answer" means that section of the case file, and page numbers are the MF Objaśnienia of 29 April 2021.

Every answer is a classification, a date or a day count, and nothing is rounded. The one money ask, `q_p11_amounts`, prints holes, since the source gives no amounts.

- **Axis.** There is one, `abolition_use` (`used` / `unused`). It reaches only `q_p11_forms`.
- **Year.** Przykłady 9 and 10 state no year, so their dates carry the assumed year 2020. The source's "31 maja" is therefore the model's `2020-05-31`.
- **Findings as rows.** Many results read the source's own findings, entered as rows: the centre of vital interests, the tie-breaker facts, and residence under a state's own law. For these asks the model reproduces the source's chain of reasoning, not the findings themselves.
- **Law.** The PIT rules and the 183 days come from the 2006 amending act (Dz.U. 2006 Nr 217 poz. 1588), the treaties from their Dziennik Ustaw publications, and the MLI change from the MLI and the two 2018 statements of its entry into force for Poland and for the UK. The first day of the credit method, `mli_from`, is now computed from those two days by MLI art. 35 ust. 1 lit. b), not entered from the synthesised text. The consolidated and synthesised texts the case quotes are their locators. No figure changed in round 2.

How `q_p08_days` gets 61 for 2019:
- The ask pins `arrived[julia] = 2019-11-01`. The case's "Missing facts" say the count "implies presence from 1 November".
- `overlap_days` counts both end days, as section 2.2 does.
- The case's check: `python3 -c "from datetime import date; print((date(2019,12,31)-date(2019,11,1)).days+1, (date(2020,5,31)-date(2020,1,1)).days+1)"` → `61 152`.
- Both keys also rest on the assumed row `last_present[julia] = 2020-05-31`.

Why `q_p08_long_stay` needs no pin:
- The ask reads the imprecise arrival `2019-11`. The 2019 count then lies between 32 and 61 days: `python3 -c "from datetime import date; print((date(2019,12,31)-date(2019,11,30)).days+1, (date(2019,12,31)-date(2019,11,1)).days+1)"` → `32 61`.
- Every value in that range is at most 183, so the comparison is decided (LANGUAGE §4.4).
- The question is the 183-day limb only. The source says nothing on Julia's centre of vital interests.
