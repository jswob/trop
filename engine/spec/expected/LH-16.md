# LH-16 expected figures

Model: `tmp/design/models/LH-16.tally`. Case file: `tmp/verification/cases/LH-16.md`. Each locator below is the case's front-matter entry (its `name`, and its `locator` in brackets). The case's `## Published answer` section samples the same cells.

**The case holds no return or CPI data.** The SBBI 1998 series are commercial (case: `data_vintage`; Transcription notes, "Data licence"). `ret`, `cpi_dec` and `cpi_avg` have no rows, so every success rate and every Table 6 statistic prints a hole with their questions. The figures below are what each ask should give once a version supplies the data. The period counts don't need the data.

**Round 2.** Annual starts are now written as key arithmetic (§4.2) with an assumed `annual_offset` of 0 months, the success shares and the Table 6 mean and median use `share`, `mean` and `quantile` (§5.10), and the abstract's range is no longer a bound: it is `abstract_rate`, whose period, payout period and success level are holes with questions (§5.17). `trinity99_v` is now known from its retrieval date, 2026-10-18 (§4.5).

**Round 3.** `trinity99_v` is now known 1999-12-31. Under §4.5, the article dated only by its year takes the earliest of three dates: the year's last day, the mirror PDF's creation (2006-05-01) and the retrieval day. The assumed `annual_offset` row moved to its own version, `trinity99_ex_v`, known from the model's `on` (2026-10-18) as a stand-in for the case's own fact. No ask has a cut, so every ask still reads both versions at `on`. No figure changes.

What every success-rate cell reads:
- **Cohorts.** One start every 12 months (`annual_start`), from `era_from` (January 1926 or January 1946) moved by `annual_offset`, assumed 0, so January starts. The last start is the one whose period ends by December 1997.
- **Withdrawals.** Each month pays `wrate`/12 of $1,000. The article's 0.25% = 3% and 1.0% = 12% (PDF p. 4) fix the /12 reading.
- **Terminal value.** Equation (1) for Tables 1, 3 and the unadjusted Table 5 blocks (`fixed`). Equation (2) for Tables 2, 4 and the adjusted Table 5 blocks (`indexed`).
- **Cell.** The share of cohorts whose terminal value passes `success`, times 100, rounded to the whole percent by `pct_round`.

The axes reached:
- `zero_end` (`fails` / `succeeds`), by every cell. A terminal value of exactly $0 is unlikely with real data, so both options should print the same cells.
- `pct_round` (`down`, `half-even`, `half-up`, `up`), by every cell. Only **`half-up`** fits the printed cells; see the derivations.
- `cpi_base` (`y1925` / `rebased`) and `cpi_value` (`december` / `annual_avg`), only by the indexed cells and Table 6 B. The case doesn't say which matches (case: Missing facts, "Equation (2) base" and "Which CPI-U value"). `y1925` is Equation (2) as printed.
- `sd_form` (`population` / `sample`), only by the standard deviation rows.
- `stat_round` (`down`, `half-even`, `half-up`), by every Table 6 figure.

**The 1946-1997 cells fit 33/28/23 periods, not the footer's 53/48/43.** Table 3, 100% Stocks, 20 years, 10% prints 61. No count of 53 periods gives 61 (32/53 = 60.38%, 33/53 = 62.26%), but 20/33 = 60.61% does. The model counts the 1946 cohorts itself (`n_annual[e1946, h]`), so it uses 33/28/23.

## Table 6 (withdrawal rates that leave $0, 60/30/10)

Each ask prints eight cells in the printed column order: 1926-1997 at 15, 20, 25 and 30 years, then 1946-1997 at the same horizons (`for r in era, h in hz`). Panel A uses `fixed` withdrawals (Equation 1) and panel B uses `indexed` ones (Equation 2).

What each figure reads:
- **The rate.** `rate0`: for each monthly cohort, the annualized rate that leaves exactly $0 at the end of the period, 12 / `need`, rounded to 1e-12. The rounding is the modeller's, forced by the 4,096-digit cap and stated in the def's note (§7.1).
- **The cohorts.** Every month from January 1926 or January 1946 whose period ends by December 1997 (`monthly_start`, PDF p. 8: "all months").
- **The statistic.** The mean, standard deviation (`sd_form`), median (`quantile` at 1/2, type 1: the smallest rate whose cumulative share reaches one half, which with an odd count is the middle cohort), minimum or maximum of `rate0` over those cohorts.
- **The rounding.** Times 100, rounded to 0.1 by `stat_round`.

## Derivations

Annual periods, 1926-1997 then 1946-1997, at 15, 20, 25 and 30 years:
`python3 -c "print([1997-1926+1-n+1 for n in (15,20,25,30)], [1997-1946+1-n+1 for n in (15,20,25,30)])"` → `[58, 53, 48, 43] [38, 33, 28, 23]`

The start-month offset. Months counted from January 1926 (0) to December 1997 (863); a start s counts if s + 12n ≤ 864. Offset 0, then 1 and 11 months:
`python3 -c "print([[len([s for s in range(k, 864, 12) if s+12*n <= 864]) for n in (15,20,25,30)] for k in (0,1,11)])"` → `[[58, 53, 48, 43], [57, 52, 47, 42], [57, 52, 47, 42]]`

Only offset 0 gives the printed 58/53/48/43, which is why the assumed `annual_offset` row is 0.

What these rule out (n = 53 at 20 years and 48 at 25 years in 1926-1997):
- **`down`.** Table 1, 100% Stocks, 20 years, 7% prints 91. The nearest count is 48/53 = 90.57%, so truncation gives 90 and `down` is out.
- **`up`.** Table 1, 100% Stocks, 20 years, 4% prints 98. Rounding up gives 98 only for 97% < 100k/53 ≤ 98%, that is 51.41 < k ≤ 51.94. No whole k fits (51 gives 96.23% and 52 gives 98.11%), so `up` is out.
- **`half-even`.** Table 1, 50/50, 25 years, 10% prints 13. The only count near it is 6/48 = 12.5%, an exact tie. Half-even gives 12 and half-up gives 13, so `half-even` is out.
