# LH-12 expected: pointers for `models/LH-12.tally`

Case file: `tmp/verification/cases/LH-12.md`. "PA" means the front matter's `published_answers` entry with that `name`. Items 1–8 of `## Published answer` repeat them.

The model has two axes:
- `lh12_mode` is the unstated rounding of the monthly pension to the grosz: `down`, `half-up` or `half-even`.
- `lh12_ok` is how an "ok." reduction is taken to whole złoty: `down` or `half-up`.

The examples rest on three `assumed` rows in `v_modeller`:
- age 60 or 65 with 0 completed months;
- application on 2026-04-01;
- the day of reaching 60 or 65 also 2026-04-01.

Versions, and the rule of LANGUAGE §4.5 that dates each one:
- `v_fus` is law, known 2017-01-05, the promulgation of Dz. U. 2017 poz. 38. That act gave art. 26 ust. 6 its 60/65 wording. The 2025 tekst jednolity is the locator.
- Each GUS table is known from its publication in Monitor Polski: `v_gus2022` on 2022-03-30, `v_gus2025` on 2025-03-28 and `v_gus2026` on 2026-03-27. Each governs the applications of 1 April to 31 March after it.
- `v_zus` (the release's examples) and `v_modeller` (the three assumptions) are stipulations, known from the model's `on` (2026-10-18).

Every ask is read at the model's `on`, so each version is visible, and no figure changes from round 1.

"Earlier table" asks pin `apply_on` and `reached_on` to 2026-03-31, the last day of the window of M.P. 2025 poz. 279 (art. 26 ust. 5).

The asks with `for e in example` print the keys in this order:
1. `w60_500`: 60-latka, 500 tys. zł;
2. `m65_500`: 65-latek, 500 tys. zł;
3. `w60_700`: 60-latka, 700 tys. zł;
4. `m65_700`: 65-latek, 700 tys. zł.

Asks with `for a in [60, 65]` print age 60, then 65.

## Calculations

1. `python3 -I -c "from decimal import Decimal as D; print(D('266.4')-D('238.9'), D('220.8')-D('196.2'), D('268.9')-D('266.4'), D('222.7')-D('220.8'))"`: the case gives the same differences (Transcription notes). Calculation 2 prints `27.5 24.6 2.5 1.9` as its last four values.
2. `python3 -I -c "from decimal import Decimal as D, ROUND_HALF_UP as H, ROUND_DOWN as R; q=D('0.01'); n=[D(c)/D(l) for c,l in ((500000,'268.9'),(500000,'222.7'),(700000,'268.9'),(700000,'222.7'))]; o=[D(c)/D(l) for c,l in ((500000,'266.4'),(500000,'220.8'),(700000,'266.4'),(700000,'220.8'))]; print([str(x.quantize(q,H)) for x in n], [str(x.quantize(q,R)) for x in n], [str(x.quantize(q,H)) for x in o], [str(x.quantize(q,R)) for x in o], [str(b.quantize(q,H)-a.quantize(q,H)) for a,b in zip(n,o)], [str(b.quantize(q,R)-a.quantize(q,R)) for a,b in zip(n,o)], D('266.4')-D('238.9'), D('220.8')-D('196.2'), D('268.9')-D('266.4'), D('222.7')-D('220.8'))"` gives, in order:
   - new pensions, half-up: `['1859.43', '2245.17', '2603.20', '3143.24']`
   - new pensions, down: `['1859.42', '2245.17', '2603.19', '3143.24']`
   - earlier pensions, half-up: `['1876.88', '2264.49', '2627.63', '3170.29']`
   - earlier pensions, down: `['1876.87', '2264.49', '2627.62', '3170.28']`
   - reductions, half-up: `['17.45', '19.32', '24.43', '27.05']`
   - reductions, down: `['17.45', '19.32', '24.43', '27.04']`
   - table differences: `27.5 24.6 2.5 1.9`

   No exact quotient is a tie at the grosz (the case's Transcription notes print them), so `half-even` gives the same as `half-up`.
