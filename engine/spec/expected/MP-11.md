## The other reading of § 185 (`fr_comp_order = bofip_text`)

The case's "Missing facts and ambiguities" says the § 140 / § 170 order differs from the example's. Its last arithmetic check gives the tax after the general cap and the excess over the first term. Under `bofip_text`, the model gives:

- `benefit[ex185]` = 1 807 × 7: `python3 -c "from decimal import Decimal as D; print(D('1807')*D('7'), D('25208')-D('1807')*D('7'))"` → `12649 12559`. So `q_benefit` prints 12 649 and `q_second_term` prints 12 559 for `ex185` under `bofip_text`. These are not published figures, so the published 14 450 and 10 758 belong to the `example` line only.
- `comp_reduction[ex185]` = min(1 801, 12 559 − 7 282), and the tax due is 12 559 minus that: `python3 -c "from decimal import Decimal as D; print(min(D('1801'), D('12559')-D('7282')), D('12559')-min(D('1801'), D('12559')-D('7282')))"` → `1801 10758`. That is the same 10 758 € as the example's order, so `q_tax_due` is `*` on the axis.

The exact quotient: `python3 -c "from fractions import Fraction as F; print(F(130000)/F('5.5'))"` → `260000/11`. The tranche sum on it, where only the 11 % tranche is reached: `python3 -c "from fractions import Fraction as F; print((F(260000,11)-11600)*F('0.11')*F('5.5'))"` → `7282`.
