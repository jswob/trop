## The three rounding readings, worked

Python's ROUND_DOWN, ROUND_HALF_UP and ROUND_CEILING are the modes `down`, `half-up` and `ceil`.


`python3 -c "from decimal import Decimal as D, ROUND_DOWN as RD, ROUND_HALF_UP as RH, ROUND_CEILING as RC; e=D('45004')/D('500000')*100; q=D('0.001'); print([(m, e.quantize(q,m), (e.quantize(q,m)*D('0.3')).quantize(q,m), D('350000')*(e.quantize(q,m)*D('0.3')).quantize(q,m)/100) for m in (RD,RH,RC)])"` → `[('ROUND_DOWN', Decimal('9.000'), Decimal('2.700'), Decimal('9450.000')), ('ROUND_HALF_UP', Decimal('9.001'), Decimal('2.700'), Decimal('9450.000')), ('ROUND_CEILING', Decimal('9.001'), Decimal('2.701'), Decimal('9453.500'))]`

The `ceil` tax, £9,453.50, is the figure the case's own notes give ("tax of £9,453.50"). The case says the printed figures "match truncation (or rounding to nearest)". At 3 decimal places, rounding to nearest gives E = 9.001% (the exact E is 9.0008%), so only truncation reproduces every printed figure. Nearest still gives the printed F and tax.
