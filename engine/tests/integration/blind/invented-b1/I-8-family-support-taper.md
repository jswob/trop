# I-8 · Family support: assessed on one year, paid over another, recomputed when the facts move

**Story.** Kasia and Leon live together with their daughter Mira, born 3 May 2019. Kasia claims family support for the period starting 1 October 2027.

- Their incomes in 2026, as the tax office first has them: Kasia 60 000.00; Leon 30 000.00, all from his job at Corvo.
- Their son Nel is born on 14 February 2028.
- Leon's job at Corvo ends on 31 January 2028. Kasia claims under rule 6 on 10 March 2028.
- Leon starts a new job on 1 July 2028 at 3 000.00 a month. They report it on 15 July 2028.
- On 12 July 2028 the support office learns that the tax office has corrected Kasia's 2026 income to 64 800.00.

**Rules.** (Family Support Act)
1. Support is paid for benefit periods running 1 October to 30 September, on the 20th of each month for that month, to the parent who claimed.
2. The family is the parents and their children under 18 who live with them. A child born in a month joins the family from the next month.
3. A month's full support is 400.00 for each child under 6 on the first day of the month and 250.00 for each child aged 6 to 17 on that day.
4. The family's monthly income is its members' income in the income year divided by 12, rounded half up to whole minor units. The income year is the calendar year that ends 9 months before the period starts.
5. If the family's monthly income exceeds 1 800.00 times the number of its members in that month, the month's support is the full support minus the excess. A result under 20.00 is not paid.
6. If a source of a member's income has ended since the income year began, the family may claim; that source's income is left out from the month after the claim.
7. If a member gains a source of income after the income year, the family must report it within 30 days. From the month after it starts, 12 times its first full month's income is added to the income year's income.
8. The office uses incomes as the tax office has them on the day the office learns them. When an income is corrected, the office recomputes every month of the period the correction reaches; support paid above what is then due is an overpayment.
9. An overpayment is withheld from later payments in the same benefit period, at most half of each. What is still owed after the payment for September is due 30 days after 30 September.

**Question.** For each month from October 2027 to September 2028: the family's members, its monthly income, and the support (a) as it would have been had the correction never come, and (b) as due after the correction. Then the overpayment, the amount withheld and the amount paid on each payday from July 2028, and anything owed after the period, with its due date.

**What makes it hard.** The support for a month reads income from a different year, and which year is set by a rule on the period, not the month. The family is a set of members that changes in the middle of the period, and the threshold moves with it. Income sources come and go, each leaving or joining the reckoning from a month fixed by a claim or by the start, not by the event. A correction learned in July rewrites months already paid, so the answer depends on what was known when, and the difference becomes a debt carried across later payments under a cap, with a remainder due after the period ends.
