# I-7 · A retirement plan across two employers: one yearly limit, a match, and a vesting clock that pauses

**Story.** Jola is paid on the last day of each month. She works for Alfa from 1 March 2025 to 30 June 2027 at 9 000.00 a month, has no job in July 2027, and works for Beta from 1 August 2027 at 11 000.00 a month.

Each employer runs a plan of the workplace kind, and Jola is a member of each while she works there. Her elections: an extra 2 % throughout at Alfa; at Beta, no extra in August and September 2027, and an extra 3 % from 1 October 2027.

| Plan | Employer pays | Match on the member's extra |
|---|---|---|
| Alfa's | 1.5 % of salary | 100 % of the extra, on at most 2 % of salary |
| Beta's | 1.5 % of salary | 50 % of the extra, on at most 4 % of salary |

She also holds a retirement account of the personal kind at Bank Orla, paying in 500.00 on the 10th of every month of 2027 and 3 000.00 on 20 December 2027.

On 20 September 2027 Alfa's plan moves her whole balance to Beta's plan. The plans hold cash only; there are no returns.

**Rules.** (Retirement Saving Act)
1. A member of a workplace plan contributes 2 % of each month's gross salary, plus any extra she elects, taken from the salary on payday. The employer pays its contribution on the same day, by its plan's terms.
2. A person's own contributions in a calendar year to all plans of the workplace and personal kinds are deducted from her taxable income, up to 9 000.00 for all plans together. Own contributions above that are refunded by 31 March of the next year, from personal-kind plans first, latest payment first. A refunded contribution is not deducted.
3. Employer contributions are taxable salary of the month they are paid.
4. Employer contributions vest all at once at the end of the last day of the 36th counted month of workplace membership. A calendar month counts if the person was a member of some workplace plan for the whole of it.
5. A member who leaves an employer before vesting keeps that plan's unvested employer contributions, and her counted months, only if the balance moves to her next employer's workplace plan within 3 months of leaving. Otherwise those contributions are forfeited on the day 3 months after leaving.
6. On a member's last day, vesting at the end of that day comes before leaving.
7. A forfeit reduces the member's taxable income of the year it happens by the amount forfeited.

**Question.**
(a) For 2027: her own contributions to each plan, the amount deducted, and the refund with its plan and latest date.
(b) On 31 Dec 2027: the employer contributions she holds, how much of them is unvested, and the date they would vest.
(c) If she leaves Beta on 29 February 2028, and again if she leaves on 31 March 2028, joining no other plan either time: the amount forfeited, the day, and the change to her 2028 taxable income.

**What makes it hard.** One payday reaches several provisions at once: her own contribution, the employer's basic part, a match whose terms belong to whichever plan she is in, a deduction under a limit shared by every plan of two kinds, and taxable salary. Membership changes kind and owner over time, and a counted-month clock carries across plans but pauses in a gap and survives only because a move came within a window. Vesting and leaving fall on one day in one branch, and their order is a rule. A forfeit years after the contributions changes a later year's income. An excess is unwound by an order over payments.
