# I-13 · Income protection: premiums deducted in some years, a benefit taxed in proportion, and an unknown return date

**Story.** Sylwia earns a salary of 7 500.00 a month, paid on the last day of each month. On 1 January 2025 she takes out an income-protection policy with the insurer Tarcza: insured income 8 000.00 a month, premium 150.00 due on the 1st of each month. From 1 January 2027 Tarcza adds a savings part to every policy it holds, and her premium becomes 170.00.

She falls ill on 10 September 2027, which is day 1 of her incapacity, and cannot work from then on. When she returns is not known; three outcomes are asked: she returns to work on 15 December 2027; on 22 May 2028; she has not returned by 31 December 2028.

**Rules.** (Social Insurance Act; Policy Act; Tarcza's terms; Income Act)
1. (Social Insurance) For days 1 to 30 of an incapacity the employer pays full salary. For days 31 to 182 the Social Insurance pays a sickness benefit of 70 % of salary. From day 183 neither pays. Salary is paid only for days on which she is not incapacitated.
2. (Social Insurance) Any amount set by the month is paid for a day as the monthly amount divided by the number of days in that month. A month's payment is the sum over its days, rounded half up to whole minor units, paid on the month's last day.
3. (Policy Act) A policy that covers only loss of income is of the protection kind. A policy with any savings part is not.
4. (Income Act) Premiums paid in a calendar year on a policy that is of the protection kind on the day of payment are deductible from that year's income, up to 1 200.00 a year. Sylwia deducts all she may.
5. (Income Act) Salary, employer pay under rule 1 and sickness benefit are taxable income of the month paid. Of a payment under an income-protection policy, the taxable part is the payment times the premiums deducted on that policy divided by all premiums paid on it, both counted up to the day of the payment, rounded half up to whole minor units; the rest is tax-free.
6. (Tarcza) The waiting period is the first 91 days of an incapacity. For each later day of incapacity Tarcza pays 60 % of the insured income, less the employer pay and sickness benefit for that day, but not below zero; day amounts and monthly payment as in rule 2.
7. (Tarcza) On each 1 January that falls within a claim, the insured income rises by 3 %, rounded half up to whole minor units.
8. (Tarcza) No premium is due for a month whose 1st falls after the waiting period and before the return to work, whether or not anything is payable under rule 6 that month. Premiums are due again from the first month whose 1st is on or after the return to work.
9. (Tarcza) Payments stop with the day before the return to work, and in any case after 730 days of incapacity after the waiting period.

**Question.** For each outcome: for each month from September 2027 to the month of return, or to December 2028 when she has not returned, her salary, employer pay, sickness benefit and Tarcza's payment; the premiums paid and waived from 2025 to 2028; the deduction for each of 2025, 2026, 2027 and 2028; the taxable part of each Tarcza payment; and her taxable income from all these sources for 2027 and 2028.

**What makes it hard.** One spell of illness is read by three payers on day counts from its first day, and the insurer's amount is reduced by what the other two pay for the same day, so it may be zero long after the waiting period ends. The policy's kind changes on a date set by the insurer's own act, and that decides whether a premium is deductible, which later decides, through a running ratio over the policy's whole life, how much of a benefit years later is taxed. A premium waiver turns on the waiting period, not on whether anything is paid. Month-based amounts are spread over days of uneven months, a yearly increase lands inside the claim, and an unknown return date gives three different histories.
