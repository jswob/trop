# I-12 · A divorce: property fixed on one date, valued on another, handed over on a third

**Story.** Paweł and Roksana married on 12 May 2018 under Lenia's shared-property regime. By a marital agreement they switched to separate property from 1 March 2023. After their divorce the court divides the former shared property by a ruling of 14 October 2027, which becomes final 21 days later; no appeal is lodged. The court's expert values everything on 30 September 2027.

| Item | History | 1 Mar 2023 | 30 Sep 2027 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat | bought 2 Jul 2019 for 500 000.00 with shared money and a joint mortgage of 300 000.00 | mortgage owed 240 000.00 | worth 640 000.00; mortgage owed 190 000.00 |
| Ceres units in Paweł's broker account | 500 bought 6 Feb 2020 for 25 000.00 with shared money; 300 bought 9 Sep 2024 for 21 000.00 with Paweł's own money | — | 72.00 each |
| Joint account | | 18 000.00 | 4 000.00 |
| Roksana's car | bought in 2016; shared money paid 6 000.00 for a new engine on 4 Apr 2021 | | worth 21 000.00 |

From 1 March 2023 to 30 September 2027 Paweł paid every mortgage instalment from his own money: 50 000.00 of principal and 41 000.00 of interest. In that time Roksana withdrew 9 000.00 from the joint account and Paweł 5 000.00.

The ruling gives Paweł the flat with its mortgage, and Roksana the 500 shared Ceres units and the joint account's balance; the units move in kind to her account on 20 November 2027. The equalisation falls due on 31 March 2028. Two outcomes are asked: it is paid on 31 March 2028; it is paid on 15 August 2028.

Roksana sells her 500 units on 17 January 2028 at 75.00. Paweł sells the flat on 1 June 2029 for 700 000.00.

**Rules.** (Family Code of Lenia; the Gains Act where named)
1. Shared property is what either spouse acquired while the shared regime held, with money of the regime. Which items are shared is fixed on the day the regime ends; their value is taken on the court's valuation date.
2. A shared item's value is its value on the valuation date minus any shared debt secured on it still owed that day. The spouse who takes the item takes that debt.
3. Money in a shared account counts at its balance on the day the regime ends. What a spouse withdrew after that day counts as already received by that spouse.
4. Each spouse is entitled to half of the total value of the shared property.
5. Shared money spent on one spouse's separate property is a claim of the shared property, at the amount spent; it counts in the total of rule 4 and as received by that spouse.
6. A spouse who paid a shared debt from separate money after the regime ended has a claim against the other spouse for half of what was paid.
7. The equalisation is the one payment that, together with the items given, money already received and the claims under rule 6, leaves each spouse with their entitlement. Late, it bears interest at 8 % a year, actual days over 365, from the day after it falls due up to and including the day it is paid, rounded half up to whole minor units.
8. (Gains Act) Receiving an item in a division is not a disposal. The half that was already the receiving spouse's keeps its original cost and acquisition date; the half received from the other spouse counts as acquired on the day the ruling becomes final, at a cost of half the item's value on the valuation date before any debt.
9. (Gains Act) A gain is proceeds minus cost; when parts of one item with different costs are sold together, the proceeds are split between them by size. A year's net gain is taxed at 19 %, due 30 April of the next year. A flat, or part of one, sold at least 5 full calendar years after the year it was acquired is exempt.

**Question.** The total value of the shared property; each spouse's entitlement; the claims under rules 5 and 6; the equalisation, who pays it to whom, and the interest in each outcome. For Roksana's sale and for Paweł's sale: each part sold, its cost and acquisition date, its gain, whether it is exempt, the tax and its due date.

**What makes it hard.** Membership of the shared estate is decided by date and by source of money, so one broker account holds shared and separate units side by side. The composition is frozen on one date, the values read on another, money is counted on the first date while withdrawals since are charged to whoever made them, and transfers happen on a third. Claims run both ways, between the estate and one spouse and between the spouses, and must net into a single payment whose lateness is an unknown with interest by days. A second regime then splits each received item into two halves with different costs and acquisition dates, so one later sale is partly exempt and partly taxed.
