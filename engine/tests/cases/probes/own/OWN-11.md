# OWN-11 · Parts that leave: the order an outflow takes them in

**Story.** At an account kept as parts (OWN-10), an outflow takes parts in an order the law writes: which owner's money a withdrawal, a tax or a sale spends. The order is a list of terms over the part k and the outflow w, compared in turn; parts still tied may share one outflow in proportion (`prorata`), but two outflows never share a part (tally 2.1, LANGUAGE §3.6, §5.14). Each part below is its own small case in 2026; the parties are Ania and Tomek (persons), `ania_tomek` (their couple) and, where named, Kuba (a child). Amounts are DUK, step 0.01; a fund account's step is 1 unit.

**The rules every part shares** (unless the part says otherwise):
- R1. An inheritance arriving at the account is the heir's; any other arrival is the couple's.
- R2. An outflow takes the couple's part first.

**P-04 · Priority, with a term on the outflow.** `sav` receives an inheritance of 2 000.00 to Ania (5 Jan) and a deposit of 500.00 (8 Jan). On 31 January the bank books, in one batch, the interest tax of 120.00 and a withdrawal of 450.00. Rule (3): among outflows the interest tax ranks first. Question: what each outflow of 31 January took from each party; the parts at its end.

**P-05 · In proportion within one outflow.** `sav` receives a deposit of 100.00 (5 Jan), an inheritance of 333.33 to Ania (6 Jan) and one of 666.67 to Tomek (7 Jan); on 25 January a withdrawal of 600.00. Rule (3): parts still tied after R2 share an outflow in proportion to their amounts, rounded to 0.01 by the largest remainder; an odd step whose remainders tie goes to Ania's part first. Question: what the withdrawal took from each party; the parts at the end of January.

**P-06 · First in, first out, with the gain per batch.** A fund account `lots` kept by batch only. Purchases: 12 units on 10 Jan for 1 260.00, 8 on 10 Feb for 920.00, 10 on 10 Mar for 1 080.00. On 15 April a sale of 15 units for 1 830.00. Rules: (1) a sale takes the oldest batch first; (2) the gain on a batch is the units the sale took of it times the sale's price a unit less the batch's cost a unit; (3) it is written once, for every sale and batch. Question: the gain on each batch, the total gain, the units left per batch at the end of April.

**P-12 · The row says whose; a shortfall goes on down the order.** `sav` receives inheritances of 600.00 to Ania (5 Jan), 400.00 to Tomek (7 Jan) and 500.00 to Kuba (8 Jan), and a deposit of 1 000.00 (6 Jan). A bank fee of 50.00 on 20 January; on 25 January a withdrawal of 900.00 whose row says Ania paid it. Rule (3): a withdrawal takes first the parts of the person its row says paid, then R2's order; a row of another kind has no such field. Question: what each outflow took from each party; the parts and the total at the end of January.

**P-19 · Refused: two outflows tied for one part** (its twin is P-04). P-04 without rule (3): the interest tax and the withdrawal tie on every term for the couple's part of 500.00, which can't cover both: refused (`RUN-TIE`), never shared or ordered by guess.

**P-20 · A hole: the field an order term reads is a hole** (its twin is P-12). P-12, but the withdrawal's row leaves the payer blank: whose money paid it is unknown. The take merges only the parts it could reach under some reading of the blank (the payer is a person: Ania or Tomek), into one part whose owner is a hole; Kuba's part, which no reading reaches, stays known, and so does the total (`HOLE-PART`). Question: the total and each party's part at the end of January.

**P-21 · Refused: an owned kind that is signed; an owned place driven below zero** (its twin is P-04). Two texts: (a) P-04 with the account kind declared signed: refused at load (`LOAD-PARTS`), since a part never goes below zero; (b) P-04 with a withdrawal of 2 450.00: the batch's outflows of 2 570.00 are more than the account's 2 500.00 (`RUN-PLACE`).

**P-22 · A hole from a cycle of rows, and its twin made a decision.** Two owned accounts: `sav` receives an inheritance of 1 000.00 to Ania (5 Jan), `acc2` one of 1 000.00 to Tomek (6 Jan). Rules: (1) money moved between the two keeps its labels; (2) an outflow takes all parts in proportion to their amounts, an odd step whose remainders tie to Ania's part first; (3) R1. (a) On 15 January the bank shows two transfers, 500.00 from `sav` to `acc2` and 300.00 from `acc2` to `sav`: rows of one day have no order, so whose money each carried is unknown and both accounts hold a hole part with their totals known; the statement of 31 January (which anchors both) states `sav` 800.00, of it Ania's 600.00 and Tomek's 200.00, and `acc2` 1 200.00, Ania's 400.00 and Tomek's 800.00, which heals it. (b) The same, but the return of 300.00 is the household's own decision taken after the first transfer: it computes exactly. Question: (a) the totals and parts at the end of 20 and 31 January; (b) the parts at the end of January.
