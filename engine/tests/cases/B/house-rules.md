# Household B · our house rules

Our own rules, in our words. Each has the date it starts and, where it changed, its versions.

## B-HR1 · The flat's costs

From 2022-03-18.
1. The flat's costs are: the mortgage's instalments, its overpayments (fees included), the building fee and the flat's insurance. We bear them 60/40, as our shares (Piotr 60 %, Ola 40 %): Ola's share is 40 % of the total, to the ort, half up, and Piotr's the rest. Electricity and everything else we use are not the flat's costs.
2. Piotr pays them from Cis. Ola sends him 1 700.00 DUK by standing order on the 5th of each month (the next business day where the 5th isn't one).
3. Once a year we work out what each of us paid of the flat's costs against our share; whoever paid less owes the other the difference. No interest.

## B-HR2 · The 26th

**v1, from 2025-01-26 to 2025-04-13.**
1. On the 26th (the next business day where the 26th isn't one), we count what we have: the Cis balance at the end of that day, with everything booked that day except this rule's own transfers, plus the florens in the Lazur account at Bank Lazur's buy rate of that day, rounded down to the ort.
2. The reserve is: a floor of 5 000.00 DUK, plus the VAT in dukats on the invoices issued so far in the month, plus 12 % of their net in dukats (to the ort), plus 4 000.00 DUK for the contributions.
3. What we have above the reserve goes, in this order: first to the mortgage as an overpayment, up to 3 000.00 DUK; the rest to the savings account.
4. Cis must keep at least 1 000.00 DUK after these transfers: if it wouldn't, the transfer to savings is cut first, then the overpayment.
5. Both transfers leave Cis the same day.

**v2, stated on 2025-04-14, as if since January.** As v1, but the floor is 8 000.00 DUK.

**v3, proposed by Ola on 2026-01-02 for the 26th from 2026-01-26; not decided.** As v2, except point 2: the reserve is the next three mortgage instalments, as they will be after this day's overpayment, plus the VAT and the 12 % of point 2, plus 4 000.00 DUK.

## B-HR3 · The overpayments, 2026–2028

Decided on 2026-01-02. We run the story's forecast for 2026 to 2028, once with B-HR2 v2's overpayments and once with nothing overpaid (all of the surplus to savings), on the same paths. If, with the overpayments, more than one path in five has the liquid money (Cis and savings) below 60 000.00 DUK at the end of any month from January 2026 to December 2028, we stop overpaying from 2026-01-26 to the end of 2028. A path's shortfall is how far its lowest month end is below 60 000.00 DUK (0 where it isn't).

## B-HR4 · How we count what we have

From 2025-01-01.
1. What's in one person's name is that person's. The flat counts at its latest valuation, split by our shares; the mortgage counts at its balance after the last instalment and any overpayment since (interest since the last instalment left out), split the same way. Ola's part is 40 %, to the ort, half up; Piotr's the rest.
2. Money owed to us counts at its gross amount while it is not yet due, promised or expected (B-HR6); a missed one counts nothing. Money we owe counts in full: taxes, contributions and VAT for periods already ended, whenever they fall due.
3. What one of us owes the other under B-HR1 counts for the one and against the other.
4. A tax return's result not yet settled counts as owed to us or by us.
5. Florens count at the CBL mid rate published for that day, or, if none was, the last one before it; each amount on its own, to the ort.

## B-HR5 · When Cis would go below zero

From 2025-01-01. If the day's payments would take Cis below zero, Piotr first moves enough from the savings account to bring Cis to 1 000.00 DUK at the end of the day, rounded up to the next 100.00 DUK.

## B-HR6 · Matching payments and what our words mean

From 2025-01-01.
1. A payment is matched to an invoice by its number in the payment's title; one that differs from what's owed by 1.00 DUK or less settles the invoice.
2. Money owed to us is "not yet due" before its due day; "promised" once whoever owes it has given a day they will pay on (a day given only as likely, "probably on …", isn't one); "expected" when it is past due and they have said they probably will pay, or will pay soon, without promising a day; "missed" when it is more than 30 days past due and we have heard nothing.
